MOSS TAG-CAPTION FORMAT
Use concise visual tags with subject attribution, following the MOSS-format caption preparation in sorryhyun/anima_lora. Produce a caption string, not layout JSON or a conversational image description.

1. Treat the image as the authority and the supplied Anima Tagger caption as evidence to check. Keep visible appearance, clothing, accessories, pose, expression, framing, and scene details. Resolve conflicting tags from the image. Do not invent identities, hidden anatomy, unseen objects, positions, or text. Do not add segmentation masks, crops, bounding boxes, or coordinates.
2. Omit rating labels (safe, general as a rating, sensitive, nsfw, questionable, explicit, sfw), quality/score/year tags, artist names, character names, franchise names, and original. Preserve count tags and descriptive appearance tags. Do not add a LoRA trigger; the application adds it separately.
3. For one visible subject, retain a compact flat caption: count, optional solo, then visible descriptive tags. Do not fabricate extra subjects or position clauses. For a scene without people, describe the actual subject or scenery without inventing a person-count tag.
4. For multiple distinct visible subjects, keep only counts and scene-wide or unassigned tags in the initial shared list. Move every subject-owned tag into that subject's position clause: appearance, outfit, expression, action, and held objects belong to their owner. Include each clearly visible person, including men, but do not invent a separate person for a POV viewpoint. Repeated depictions of the same character may each have a clause.
5. Write position clauses as sentences after the shared list: ". On the left, ...", ". On the middle, ...", ". On the right, ...". Use visible relative positions, including top/bottom when needed. A period separates clauses; commas separate tags inside one clause. Repeat a shared attribute inside each owning subject's clause when it applies to several subjects. Do not leave a subject-owned tag in the shared list as well.
6. Remove a generic tag when a more specific tag already describes the SAME attribute of the SAME subject or item: very long hair replaces long hair; red bowtie replaces bow and neckerchief when they describe that knot; white dress replaces dress. Keep distinct items such as a hair bow alongside a bowtie. Negation does not imply its positive: no pants must not become pants. Remove exact duplicates within each subject's clause. Never remove a tag from one subject merely because another subject has a more specific tag, and never globally deduplicate repeated tags across different subject clauses.
7. Keep different simultaneous properties that are visibly compatible. Resolve a genuine eye-color or hair-color conflict per subject, while preserving explicit multicolored hair or heterochromia when supported. Do not collapse multiple visible garments to one clothing label.
8. Preserve supplied, legible text clauses verbatim and last, including their quoted punctuation. Do not guess or translate unreadable text. Do not add OCR coordinates.
9. Use lowercase tag names and the canonical capitalized "On the" clause headers. Do not add emphasis weights, markdown, commentary, or prose outside the caption. Return the full caption in the requested positive JSON field.
