PURPOSE

Expand ONLY the assigned scenes into one single-image MOSS layout each, writing every image description in fluent, complete natural-language English sentences. Scene selection, selected registered identities and resolved Profiles are fixed. Each input scene contains its anchor/slot and selected characters' active Profiles. An empty input characters array means no registered identity is selected, not that the scene is missing. Depict its source-grounded NPCs or creatures using character items below, or omit character items for an event or environmental view with no separate subjects. Preserve the selected event without inserting a registered observer or borrowing a Profile. Its brief may contain a short selection intent or an expanded visual plan. Tracked outfit/hair state and tracked time/place are supplied only when their independent preparation stages are enabled. Treat every scene independently against its own source chronology; preserve established continuity without copying incompatible wardrobe, Profile, camera or context from another scene.

For source-established unregistered participants, follow the brief's choice of an anonymous fragment or a separately depicted person. If that choice is unspecified, decide from the source event, intended focus and active image policy, not the number of supplied names. An assigned single-subject view means the brief and active policy select one independently depicted person, registered or NPC; it is not inferred from the number of registered input entries. A shared view of one registered person and one separately depicted NPC remains a two-person view. For an assigned single-subject view, including such views within a mixed batch, first decide whether visible partner contact is necessary to communicate the selected moment. Every independently depicted NPC uses a character item, including an NPC selected as the sole subject under SINGLE. Use a concise source-grounded role label distinct from registered names; derive its visible traits from the source or prepared brief without borrowing a registered Profile or LoRA. Do not add an unassigned registered identity as an NPC. A fragment stays in `summary.caption` and receives no separate item or bbox.

- `bbox_coord_space`: exactly `"abs_centralized"`.
- `bbox_format`: exactly `"yxyx"`.

Each item is `{"label": "character"|"object", "name": "...", "caption": "...", "bbox": [y0, x0, y1, x1]}`.

Object captions describe only that object's visible design and state in concise natural English.
- `bbox` is four integers in the supplied image's pixel coordinates.

COORDINATE CONTRACT

The bbox coordinate space is the same one the native MOSS layout node uses: `abs_centralized` with `yxyx`.
- The origin `(0, 0)` is the EXACT CENTER of the image.
- The y axis grows DOWNWARD. The x axis grows RIGHTWARD.
- `bbox = [y0, x0, y1, x1]`: the first pair is the top-left corner offset from the image center, and the second pair is the bottom-right corner offset. `y0` and `x0` are usually negative; `y1` and `x1` are usually positive.
- For an image of width W and height H, valid coordinates are `-floor(H/2) <= y <= floor(H/2)` and `-floor(W/2) <= x <= floor(W/2)`. The full canvas is `[-floor(H/2), -floor(W/2), floor(H/2), floor(W/2)]`.
- `y0 < y1` and `x0 < x1` are required. Emit integers, never normalized 0..1 values and never percentages.
- Compute each bbox from the supplied `image_size` and the visible body/object extent. Do not copy coordinates from another scene or from a default rectangle.
- Keep character bboxes tight around the visible body, hair and worn silhouette plus connected limbs needed for the pose. Keep object bboxes tight around the object's visible extent. Never split physically stacked, pinning, straddling, embracing or directly touching participants into disjoint left/right halves just to keep boxes separate: when one person is directly on top of or leaning over another, stack and overlap their boxes to match their real projected silhouettes (the upper/foreground person's box sits higher with upper.y0 < lower.y0 and shares substantial horizontal overlap across the lower person's box, while the lower person's box sits beneath), and in tight contact close-ups overlap both actors' boxes directly across the shared contact zone.
