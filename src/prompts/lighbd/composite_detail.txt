HORIZONTAL PLACEMENT
Decide ONLY the horizontal position of each character. Output x as the character cutout's horizontal CENTER in normalized background coordinates: 0 is the left edge, 0.5 the middle, and 1 the right edge. Do not output mask, y, width, height, scale, or z. Final assembly trims fully transparent margins, scales the character proportionally so its height equals the entire background height, and places its top at y=0 and bottom at the background's bottom edge. Width follows from the character's aspect ratio; it must never reduce the full-height scale. Content extending beyond the left or right edge is clipped by the canvas.

Place characters from the depicted interaction, never from their array index. Each layer's facing and gaze are baked into that character's own image, so x must be chosen to make that gaze land where the scene intends. Before choosing x, decide for every visible pair who looks at, speaks to, or physically engages whom, then:
- A character whose gaze or face turns toward screen-LEFT must sit to the RIGHT of the person they look at; a character whose gaze turns toward screen-RIGHT must sit to the LEFT of that person. Two characters looking at each other therefore face inward: the left-looking one is on the right, the right-looking one on the left. For example, if Alice's tags say `looking to the left` toward Bob, give Alice a larger x than Bob.
- Keep the source gaze direction and move x instead. Never rewrite or mirror a character's gaze in positive to justify a position, and never assign x by array order.
- When a character looks toward someone or something outside the frame, place them toward the opposite side of the background so their gaze travels across the image instead of immediately off the edge.
- When nobody interacts, follow the source staging: speaker and listener roles, entrance direction, foreground/background depth, and reading order.
- Separate x centers enough that no character's face is covered by another drawn later in the array, and keep a mutually facing pair roughly symmetric around the frame center unless the source stages them otherwise. Do not try to make a character smaller or float it above the bottom. This same rule applies to every scene and Key Visual.
