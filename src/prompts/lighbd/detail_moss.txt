PURPOSE

Expand ONLY the assigned scenes into one single-image MOSS layout each. Scene selection, selected registered identities and resolved Profiles are fixed. Each input scene contains its anchor/slot and selected characters' active Profiles. An empty input characters array means no registered identity is selected, not that the scene is missing. Depict its source-grounded NPCs or creatures using character items below, or omit character items for an event or environmental view with no separate subjects. Preserve the selected event without inserting a registered observer or borrowing a Profile. Its brief may contain a short selection intent or an expanded visual plan. Tracked outfit/hair state and tracked time/place are supplied only when their independent preparation stages are enabled. Treat every scene independently against its own source chronology; preserve established continuity without copying incompatible wardrobe, Profile, camera or context from another scene.

For source-established unregistered participants, follow the brief's choice of an anonymous fragment or a separately depicted person. If that choice is unspecified, decide from the source event, intended focus and active image policy, not the number of supplied names. A hand offering an object can remain a fragment; a visible exchange of reactions may call for a separate NPC. An assigned single-subject view means the brief and active policy select one independently depicted person, registered or NPC; it is not inferred from the number of registered input entries. A shared view of one registered person and one separately depicted NPC remains a two-person view. For an assigned single-subject view, including such views within a mixed batch, first decide whether visible partner contact is necessary to communicate the selected moment. If the selected subject's own gesture, gaze or expression communicates it, keep the partner entirely off-frame. Being addressed, watched or mentioned does not by itself require a visible partner. Apply this single-subject visibility decision even when the brief describes the partner's appearance or a body area being looked at; preserve the directed gaze or reaction without automatically putting its target in frame. When contact is essential, show only the smallest connected anonymous fragment needed to explain it, without a separate character entry, identifiable face, full silhouette or additional person-count tag. Describe that fragment's concrete visible extent, frame-edge entry and contact with the selected subject; do not describe its owner as another independently posed figure or add their identifying traits and reactions. The selected cast and visible extent must agree across all image text: a one-entry roster or solo tag cannot cancel a second actor described in prose. Every independently depicted NPC uses a character item, including an NPC selected as the sole subject under SINGLE. A secondary NPC subject appears only when the selected view, active policy and character limit permit multiple independently depicted people. Use a concise source-grounded role label distinct from registered names; derive its visible traits from the source or prepared brief without borrowing a registered Profile or LoRA. Do not add an unassigned registered identity as an NPC. A fragment stays in `summary.caption` and receives no separate item or bbox.

SCENE AND STATE

Resolve the concrete depicted space and each person's current posture, support and contact from this anchor's source chronology, earlier context and any supplied brief. Name the specific posture together with what supports the body; merely listing a floor or wall as scenery does not establish that relation. Preserve source-established distinctions between postures and their supporting surfaces instead of reducing them to generic sitting or adding a new movement. Keep visible or framing-defining support in the composition even when the background is kept simple. When supplied, each character's `time_place.time`, `time_place.place` and `time_place.lighting` belong to this same SLOT and establish its physical setting and illumination. A broad label does not replace or limit a more specific source-grounded setting. Derive the concrete setting from the source and the supplied genre/world instructions, and express that world through a few visible architectural features, materials, furnishings or fixtures that fit this specific location, never as a genre or era tag. Describe the setting once in `summary.caption`; every item in the scene shares that same place and light relationship. Preserve the tracked light source and its relation to the space in the shared scene description; a different crop may show different shadows but does not create a different illumination condition. Retain the source-grounded interior/exterior and light-entry relationship even when the source itself is off-frame: window light entering a room must not become outdoor sunlight or generic decorative light spots. Do not add a sunset, colored glow or new light source for mood or variety. Recover missing or `unknown` aspects from the source and earlier context even when no brief is supplied; neither a missing field nor `unknown` means darkness or becomes an image tag. Do not invent a room, furnishing, prop or activity to fill a gap, or merge incompatible locations or story instants. Setting refinement does not change outfit, hair or the chosen event.

When the brief is absent or contains only a short selection intent, use the assigned anchor's original passage and its adjacent CURRENT passages to resolve the visible action and the people involved, preserving any supplied selection intent. Earlier history explains only facts that still hold at this instant; a completed action, released contact or superseded pose must not be replayed as the current event. Develop one source-supported visible instant there, keeping the latest established body support and ongoing activity until the source changes them, then decide its camera, crop, composition, pose and contact. Do not substitute another event from the history or a plausible activity associated with the location. Keep the assigned scene count, anchors and selected registered participants unchanged. Distinguish scenes through supported poses, actions, reactions and contact changes, not invisible thoughts or abstract effects. Establish who acts on what, body support and connection, acting-surface orientation and necessary contact/depth relations under one coherent viewpoint. Preserve the trusted bot policy's sole independently depicted subject and minimum connected anonymous partner fragment where applicable, regardless of the subject's registration. Client focus and direction guide only these assigned scenes.

When the supplied brief includes staging details, preserve that scene's specified event, actor/receiver, camera, crop, composition, pose, spatial relations, contact point and direction, and source-established setting. Fill unspecified details from the source, and make only the minimum source-faithful correction if specified geometry is physically impossible, without choosing a different event or instant. For each registered character, the supplied `profile` is the immutable resolved Profile at this anchor. When `state` is supplied, that person's `state.outfit` and `state.hair` are the complete authoritative tags already decided by the state tracker and filled by the server for this SLOT. Do not override supplied tracked wardrobe/hair, add Profile default garments to that state, merge older/later states or invent a changed hairstyle. An empty state field contains no tags and does not authorize a default fallback. When `state` is absent, determine current outfit and hair from source chronology at this scene's own anchor, using prior saved memory and earlier context; apply Profile defaults only when narrative and memory are silent. Missing tracking is not an empty outfit and never establishes nudity. Profile appearance supplies other fixed traits; apply the face/eye preservation rule below, while supplied SLOT hair state owns current hair.

Across scenes separated by only a short interval of story time, keep the same established garments, colors and design unless the source establishes an actual clothing change. The same garments may become wet, rumpled, torn, opened, displaced or removed as events support; these changes affect their condition or worn state, not their identity. A new scene, pose, crop or location alone does not justify replacing the outfit or resetting it to Profile defaults.

For the current outfit established by either path, select visible or coverage-defining tags while preserving garment identity, colors and exposure. Read tags describing one garment together as one design. For its visible regions, retain the supplied shape, length, openings, drape and layering that determine its silhouette and coverage; a generic garment name does not replace those features. Keep the supplied terms for retained garment features, including unfamiliar ones, rather than guessing replacement tags. Omit features outside the crop or naturally occluded, and unreadable incidental decoration, without changing the visible garment. For example, a portrait can omit hidden trouser cuffs, while a visible jacket keeps its established length and open or closed front. Do not invent garments, patterns, trim, fastenings or accessories, or redesign the outfit to simplify it.

FACE AND EYE IDENTITY

Preserve the selected Profile's supplied face and eye identity tags verbatim in that character's `caption`, using `face_tags`, `eye_tags` and facial/eye traits in `appearance`. Combine facial/eye identity tags from all supplied lists: `face_tags` and `eye_tags` are additional render references, never replacements or an exhaustive whitelist for facial/eye tags in `appearance`. A generic eye or pupil description does not replace a supplied specific symbol. Preserve a supplied facial/eye token even if it resembles an expression; choose a compatible generated expression instead of deleting the token. Preserve symbolic or unfamiliar spellings and exact weights; never guess a replacement tag, paraphrase them or treat them as disposable decoration. A supplied current SLOT hair state still supersedes hairstyle defaults embedded in `face_tags`. Add scene-supported expression and gaze alongside the fixed traits: narrowed or half-lidded eyes do not erase pupil identity. Omit a trait only when its own facial/eye region is wholly outside the frame or naturally fully occluded; do not omit it for shot scale, prompt compression or uncertain tag meaning.

ABSOLUTE TAGS AND COMPLETE CHARACTER SETTINGS

Read the selected Profile's appearance, face_tags, eye_tags, eye_prompt and absolute_tags together with its current outfit and hair. appearance is not an exhaustive whitelist: the other fields add settings and never replace more specific supplied traits. Apply eye_prompt as the owning character's eye-description constraint. Include absolute_tags verbatim in every character-item caption depicting that character, subject to the explicit current-state and character_negative precedence above/below; they are required positive tags, not optional background context. Reference pictures supplement the supplied settings and do not authorize omitting visible identity or wardrobe. Each occurrence needs its own complete visible settings, including repeated shots of the same person. Before returning, compare every character description against all supplied fields and restore missing visible traits without adding unsupported ones; do not output this check.

CHARACTER EMOTION AND VISIBLE EXPRESSION

For each named character separately, follow that person's emotional progression in the source chronology up to the chosen instant. Resolve who feels, speaks, reacts and is being observed from context; a narrator's feelings or one participant's reaction do not belong to everyone in the scene. Earlier context explains the current reaction, but an earlier expression does not persist automatically and a later realization must not appear early. Use the supplied brief with the source at this anchor, not another scene's interpretation or the character's usual temperament as a fixed expression.

Distinguish what the person feels from what they visibly show, including deliberate restraint or concealment. Make that outward reaction readable through a concise, coherent combination of brows, eyelids, gaze and mouth shape, with head/shoulder tension or gesture when useful. Choose the features that distinguish this moment rather than listing every facial part, stacking emotion labels or relying on blush alone. A restrained smile with tense eyes differs from relaxed delight; a startled interruption differs from a shy smile. These are examples, not fixed emotion-to-tag mappings. Match the source's intensity, allow quiet or neutral expressions when supported, and never add a smile or exaggerate reactions just to make the batch varied. Describe one instantaneous expression, not its before-and-after sequence. Keep each person's cues in their own `caption`; a shared caption must unambiguously bind any expressive relation to its visible owner.

For a reaction-led scene with camera details still open, choose framing and head direction that let the important expression read. Preserve supplied camera, essential action/contact and natural occlusion; if the face is hidden or too small, convey the supported reaction through readable body language instead of forcing facial microdetail into the prompt. Translate emotional metaphors into literal visible behavior, never invented objects or wearable items; preserve actual source- or state-established items.

CHARACTER EXCLUSIONS

Exclusions take precedence over inclusion of conflicting Profile appearance, face/eye identity tags, brief details or tracked state tags; omit only the conflicting trait while preserving the remaining source facts and state. Apply each exclusion only to its owning character and selected Profile, never to other people. Do not invent an opposite trait or rewrite stored character/state data. These are description constraints: do not copy them into `negative`, turn them into negated captions, or treat them as instructions to the image generator. An absent or empty `character_negative` adds no exclusion.

TAGS AND NATURAL LANGUAGE

MOSS reads tags and natural-language captions as one conditioning prompt. Include detail that establishes readable identity, current state or the chosen visible event; other detail supplied in the brief/profile is not a requirement to enumerate it; supplied face and eye identity tags follow the preservation rule above. Use compact tags for visible identity, current wardrobe and the main pose/action, removing repetition and incidental refinements without dropping the visible garment structure defined above. Preserve the brief's action-defining support, body connection, surface orientation and target relation; these are not incidental posture nuances. Let the image model realize nonessential joint/finger refinements, fabric folds and decoration.

Source-established injury marks (wounds, bruises and scars) get only concise, non-redundant type/body-region tags in the affected character's `caption`. For this injury-only reduction, treat detailed injury narration in a prepared brief as reference to shorten, not wording to preserve. Keep its event, pose and camera geometry, but put no description of injury appearance or anatomical landmarks in `summary.caption`. In the shared caption, mention injuries only to explain a necessary physical action, contact or occlusion; preserve treatment mechanics without amplifying injury appearance.

Alongside the shared setting and framing, use coherent English in `summary.caption` to carry the chosen instant's source-grounded physical relations that compact tags do not express clearly: who acts on what, body support and connection, the acting surface's orientation toward its target, and necessary contact or occlusion. Use one connected description of the chosen instant, with enough context to bind the action to the right body or object. State each relation once where it reads best; do not repeat the same action across shared and item captions or recaption appearance, clothing, expression, lighting and scenery already conveyed. For an ongoing transfer, express the incoming/contact path and the receiving surface's direction explicitly; a held result or outgoing spill alone does not describe that transfer. Preserve these concrete relations from the source or supplied brief rather than reducing them back to an action label. A simple portrait may need only a gaze target or no additional interaction description. Do not fill missing relationships with decorative detail or transfer omitted microdetails into prose. Let the scene determine the length, with no fixed tag count, ratio or sentence quota.

In `summary.caption`, identify people by their actual visible placement or pose, with a minimal established distinguishing trait if needed, rather than narrative names. Use one consistent referent for each visible person. Bind that person's actions, expression and gaze to the same referent, without wording that suggests another actor. For a single visible subject, prefer direct pose-and-action phrasing. Describe a gaze toward an off-frame participant through its visible direction, without introducing that participant as another figure. When multiple people are intentionally visible, keep their references and actions distinct. Do not invent a placement to replace a name. Identify an anonymous fragment by its visible region and frame entry, without assigning an identity. Keep required image identity/LoRA trigger tokens intact.

OUTPUT FIELDS

The `scenes` array contains exactly one scene for EACH assigned slot. Each scene has:
- `slot`: the exact supplied Cxxx anchor string.
- `image_size`: `{"width": W, "height": H}` copied from the supplied scene. Use this size for every bbox in the scene.
- `summary`: `{"caption": "..."}` containing the shared background, environment, lighting, framing and composition for the whole image. Include the source-established location, time, relevant weather, light source/direction and visible features that make the place identifiable; omit unspecified details instead of inventing them; do not substitute a blank, plain or generic gradient for a source-grounded setting. Keep individual identity, wardrobe and pose detail in the character item captions; keep shared contact, placement and object relationships in this summary.
- `items`: an array of layout items. Every independently depicted character is one item with `label: "character"`, its registered canonical name or source-grounded NPC role label in `name`, and its final visible image description in `caption`. Every visible separate object, prop, garment piece, furniture, weapon or held item whose position matters is an additional item with `label: "object"` and a concise visible `caption`. Do not create object items for broad scenery, background texture or a surface that has no compact visible extent; those belong in `summary.caption`. It is correct to bbox small and incidental visible objects when they have a distinct position.
- `bbox_coord_space`: exactly `"abs_centralized"`.
- `bbox_format`: exactly `"yxyx"`.

Each item is `{"label": "character"|"object", "name": "...", "caption": "...", "bbox": [y0, x0, y1, x1]}`.
- `name` is required for characters: copy registered names exactly from the assigned scene roster, and use a stable source-grounded role label for an unregistered NPC. The full registered reference pool is not an instruction to depict everyone. For objects use a short stable role label if useful; it is not an identity.
- `caption` is required and non-empty. Character captions combine that person's visible identity, current wardrobe, pose, action, expression and gaze using compact tags and clear English where useful. Object captions describe only that object's visible design and state.
- `bbox` is four integers in the supplied image's pixel coordinates.

COORDINATE CONTRACT

The bbox coordinate space is the same one the native MOSS layout node uses: `abs_centralized` with `yxyx`.
- The origin `(0, 0)` is the EXACT CENTER of the image.
- The y axis grows DOWNWARD. The x axis grows RIGHTWARD.
- `bbox = [y0, x0, y1, x1]`: the first pair is the top-left corner offset from the image center, and the second pair is the bottom-right corner offset. `y0` and `x0` are usually negative; `y1` and `x1` are usually positive.
- For an image of width W and height H, valid coordinates are `-floor(H/2) <= y <= floor(H/2)` and `-floor(W/2) <= x <= floor(W/2)`. The full canvas is `[-floor(H/2), -floor(W/2), floor(H/2), floor(W/2)]`.
- `y0 < y1` and `x0 < x1` are required. Emit integers, never normalized 0..1 values and never percentages.
- Compute each bbox from the supplied `image_size` and the visible body/object extent. Do not copy coordinates from another scene or from a default rectangle.
- Keep character bboxes tight around the visible body, hair and worn silhouette plus connected limbs needed for the pose. Keep object bboxes tight around the object's visible extent. It is correct for boxes to overlap when contact or depth requires it.
