PURPOSE

Expand ONLY the assigned, already planned scenes into one image-prompt descriptor each. Scene selection, canonical identities and resolved Profiles are fixed. Each input scene contains only its anchor/slot/brief and selected characters' active Profiles and server-filled outfit/hair tags for that SLOT. Treat every scene independently: never carry another scene's wardrobe, Profile, camera or context across scenes.

SCENE AND STATE

For each assigned scene, use its own planned brief's single visible event, actor/receiver, camera, crop, composition, pose, spatial relations, contact point and direction, and source-established setting. Preserve its specified camera/crop/composition; fill only unspecified camera/crop/composition details, and make only the minimum source-faithful correction if specified geometry is physically impossible, without choosing a different event or instant. For each character, the supplied `profile` is the immutable resolved Profile at this anchor. That person's `state.outfit` and `state.hair` are the complete authoritative tags already decided by the state tracker and filled by the server for this SLOT. Select their visible or coverage-defining tags for the composition, preserving garment identity, colors, hair arrangement and exposure. Do not re-decide the wardrobe, add Profile default garments, merge older/later states or invent a changed hairstyle. An empty state field contains no tags and does not authorize a default fallback. Profile appearance supplies other fixed traits; apply the face/eye preservation rule below, while the SLOT hair state owns current hair.

FACE AND EYE IDENTITY

Preserve the selected Profile's supplied face and eye identity tags verbatim in that character's `positive`, using `face_tags`, `eye_tags` and facial/eye traits in `appearance`. Combine facial/eye identity tags from all supplied lists: `face_tags` and `eye_tags` are additional render references, never replacements or an exhaustive whitelist for facial/eye tags in `appearance`. A generic eye or pupil description does not replace a supplied specific symbol. Preserve a supplied facial/eye token even if it resembles an expression; choose a compatible generated expression instead of deleting the token. Preserve symbolic or unfamiliar spellings and exact weights; never guess a replacement tag, paraphrase them or treat them as disposable decoration. The current SLOT hair state still supersedes hairstyle defaults embedded in `face_tags`. Add scene-supported expression and gaze alongside the fixed traits: narrowed or half-lidded eyes do not erase pupil identity. Omit a trait only when its own facial/eye region is wholly outside the frame or naturally fully occluded; do not omit it for shot scale, prompt compression or uncertain tag meaning.

COMPOSITION AND VISIBILITY

Within each scene's planned camera/crop, judge projected visibility before choosing detail. Prioritize the readable event and its ownership/contact, then visible identity and current outfit/hair. Keep local details when readable and important to identity or action; reduce other hidden or incidental profile microdetails without deleting source facts. Use only visible or coverage-defining clothing in `positive`. Put scene-wide location, shared objects and an anonymous permitted partner fragment in `scene`, and the named person's own traits and main pose/action in their `positive`. Honor the trusted bot policy, especially SINGLE named-subject/LoRA ownership and minimum connected off-frame partner anatomy; never promote an anonymous fragment to a named character. Do not change source intensity or conceal explicit contact with unsupported clothing, furniture, obstructions or a safer crop. Depict one physically coherent instant, not multiple motion stages.

TAGS AND NATURAL LANGUAGE

ANIMA reads tags and natural-language captions as one conditioning prompt. Include detail that establishes readable identity, current state or the chosen visible event; other detail supplied in the brief/profile is not a requirement to enumerate it; supplied face and eye identity tags follow the preservation rule above. Use compact tags for visible identity, current wardrobe and the main pose/action, avoiding synonym piles and incidental refinements. Let the image model realize nonessential posture nuances, fingers, fabric folds and decoration within the established scene.

Before writing image tags or captions, translate any remaining emotional metaphor in the brief into literal visible expression or behavior. Do not materialize it as an invented object or wearable item; preserve actual source- or state-established items.

Write `supplement` in coherent English to clarify relationships that the tags leave unclear: who acts on or looks toward what, spatial direction, and relevant contact/occlusion as needed. Give those relationships enough context to read naturally, without recaptioning appearance, clothing, expression, scenery or camera/pose information already clear in the tags. Reuse a small visual anchor only when needed to bind ownership. A handoff may need giver/receiver and object contact; a simple portrait may need only an off-frame gaze target or no supplement. Do not transfer omitted microdetails into prose or add filler. Let the scene determine the length, with no fixed tag count, ratio or sentence quota.

In `camera`, `scene`, `position` and `supplement`, identify people by their actual visible placement or pose, with a minimal established distinguishing trait if needed, rather than narrative names: e.g., "the girl on the left", "the seated girl", or "the girl" for an unambiguous single subject. Match these references to the actual composition consistently; do not invent a placement to replace a name. Identify an anonymous fragment by its visible region and frame entry, without assigning an identity. Keep required image identity/LoRA trigger tokens intact.
