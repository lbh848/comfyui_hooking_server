PURPOSE

Expand ONLY the assigned scenes into one image-prompt descriptor each. Scene selection, selected registered identities and resolved Profiles are fixed. Each input scene contains its anchor/slot and selected characters' active Profiles. Its brief may contain a short selection intent or an expanded visual plan. Tracked outfit/hair state and tracked time/place are supplied only when their independent preparation stages are enabled. Treat every scene independently: never carry another scene's wardrobe, Profile, camera or context across scenes.

For source-established unregistered participants, follow the brief's choice of an anonymous fragment or a separately depicted person. If that choice is unspecified, decide from the source event, intended focus and active image policy, not the number of supplied names. A hand offering an object can remain a fragment; a visible exchange of reactions may call for a separate NPC. Under SINGLE keep only the minimum connected anonymous fragment, without a separate character entry, face, full silhouette or additional person-count tag. Otherwise a separately depicted NPC uses an ordinary character entry within the supplied character limit. Use a concise source-grounded role label distinct from registered names; derive its visible traits from the source or prepared brief without borrowing a registered Profile or LoRA. Do not add an unassigned registered identity as an NPC. A fragment stays in the shared scene description and receives no character entry or region.

SCENE AND STATE

Resolve the concrete depicted space and each person's current posture, support and contact from this anchor's source chronology, earlier context and any supplied brief. Name the specific posture together with what supports the body; merely listing a floor or wall as scenery does not establish that relation. Preserve source-established distinctions between postures and their supporting surfaces instead of reducing them to generic sitting or adding a new movement. Keep visible or framing-defining support in the composition even with a minimal background. When supplied, each character's `time_place.time`, `time_place.place` and `time_place.lighting` belong to this same SLOT and establish its physical setting and illumination. A broad label does not replace or limit a more specific source-grounded setting. Preserve the tracked light source and its relation to the space in the shared scene description; a different crop may show different shadows but does not create a different illumination condition. Retain the source-grounded interior/exterior and light-entry relationship even when the source itself is off-frame: window light entering a room must not become outdoor sunlight or generic decorative light spots. Do not add a sunset, colored glow or new light source for mood or variety. Recover missing or `unknown` aspects from the source and earlier context even when no brief is supplied; neither a missing field nor `unknown` means darkness or becomes an image tag. Do not invent a room, furnishing, prop or activity to fill a gap, or merge incompatible locations or story instants. Setting refinement does not change outfit, hair or the chosen event.

When the brief is absent or contains only a short selection intent, use the assigned anchor's original passage and its adjacent CURRENT passages to resolve the visible action and the people involved, preserving any supplied selection intent. Earlier history explains only facts that still hold at this instant; a completed action, released contact or superseded pose must not be replayed as the current event. Develop one source-supported visible instant there, keeping the latest established body support and ongoing activity until the source changes them, then decide its camera, crop, composition, pose and contact. Do not substitute another event from the history or a plausible activity associated with the location. Keep the assigned scene count, anchors and selected registered participants unchanged. Distinguish scenes through supported poses, actions, reactions and contact changes, not invisible thoughts or abstract effects. Establish who acts on what, body support and connection, acting-surface orientation and necessary contact/depth relations under one coherent viewpoint. Preserve the trusted bot policy's sole named subject and minimum connected anonymous partner fragment where applicable. Client focus and direction guide only these assigned scenes.

When the supplied brief includes staging details, preserve that scene's specified event, actor/receiver, camera, crop, composition, pose, spatial relations, contact point and direction, and source-established setting. Fill unspecified details from the source, and make only the minimum source-faithful correction if specified geometry is physically impossible, without choosing a different event or instant. For each registered character, the supplied `profile` is the immutable resolved Profile at this anchor. When `state` is supplied, that person's `state.outfit` and `state.hair` are the complete authoritative tags already decided by the state tracker and filled by the server for this SLOT. Do not override supplied tracked wardrobe/hair, add Profile default garments to that state, merge older/later states or invent a changed hairstyle. An empty state field contains no tags and does not authorize a default fallback. When `state` is absent, determine current outfit and hair from source chronology at this scene's own anchor, using prior saved memory and earlier context; apply Profile defaults only when narrative and memory are silent. Missing tracking is not an empty outfit and never establishes nudity. Profile appearance supplies other fixed traits; apply the face/eye preservation rule below, while supplied SLOT hair state owns current hair.

For the current outfit established by either path, select visible or coverage-defining tags while preserving garment identity, colors and exposure. Read tags describing one garment together as one design. For its visible regions, retain the supplied shape, length, openings, drape and layering that determine its silhouette and coverage; a generic garment name does not replace those features. Keep the supplied terms for retained garment features, including unfamiliar ones, rather than guessing replacement tags. Omit features outside the crop or naturally occluded, and unreadable incidental decoration, without changing the visible garment. For example, a portrait can omit hidden trouser cuffs, while a visible jacket keeps its established length and open or closed front. Do not invent garments, patterns, trim, fastenings or accessories, or redesign the outfit to simplify it.

FACE AND EYE IDENTITY

Preserve the selected Profile's supplied face and eye identity tags verbatim in that character's `positive`, using `face_tags`, `eye_tags` and facial/eye traits in `appearance`. Combine facial/eye identity tags from all supplied lists: `face_tags` and `eye_tags` are additional render references, never replacements or an exhaustive whitelist for facial/eye tags in `appearance`. A generic eye or pupil description does not replace a supplied specific symbol. Preserve a supplied facial/eye token even if it resembles an expression; choose a compatible generated expression instead of deleting the token. Preserve symbolic or unfamiliar spellings and exact weights; never guess a replacement tag, paraphrase them or treat them as disposable decoration. A supplied current SLOT hair state still supersedes hairstyle defaults embedded in `face_tags`. Add scene-supported expression and gaze alongside the fixed traits: narrowed or half-lidded eyes do not erase pupil identity. Omit a trait only when its own facial/eye region is wholly outside the frame or naturally fully occluded; do not omit it for shot scale, prompt compression or uncertain tag meaning.

CHARACTER EMOTION AND VISIBLE EXPRESSION

For each named character separately, follow that person's emotional progression in the source chronology up to the chosen instant. Resolve who feels, speaks, reacts and is being observed from context; a narrator's feelings or one participant's reaction do not belong to everyone in the scene. Earlier context explains the current reaction, but an earlier expression does not persist automatically and a later realization must not appear early. Use the supplied brief with the source at this anchor, not another scene's interpretation or the character's usual temperament as a fixed expression.

Distinguish what the person feels from what they visibly show, including deliberate restraint or concealment. Make that outward reaction readable through a concise, coherent combination of brows, eyelids, gaze and mouth shape, with head/shoulder tension or gesture when useful. Choose the features that distinguish this moment rather than listing every facial part, stacking emotion labels or relying on blush alone. A restrained smile with tense eyes differs from relaxed delight; a startled interruption differs from a shy smile. These are examples, not fixed emotion-to-tag mappings. Match the source's intensity, allow quiet or neutral expressions when supported, and never add a smile or exaggerate reactions just to make the batch varied. Describe one instantaneous expression, not its before-and-after sequence. Keep each person's cues in their own `positive`; a shared caption must unambiguously bind any expressive relation to its visible owner.

For a reaction-led scene with camera details still open, choose framing and head direction that let the important expression read. Preserve supplied camera, essential action/contact and natural occlusion; if the face is hidden or too small, convey the supported reaction through readable body language instead of forcing facial microdetail into the prompt. Translate emotional metaphors into literal visible behavior, never invented objects or wearable items; preserve actual source- or state-established items.

CHARACTER EXCLUSIONS

The selected Profile's `character_negative` lists user-specified traits to exclude from that character's image description. Interpret their meaning in context, including equivalent wording; do not describe those traits in this character's positive tags or anywhere else depicting that character, including `scene` and `supplement`. Exclusions take precedence over inclusion of conflicting Profile appearance, face/eye identity tags, brief details or tracked state tags; omit only the conflicting trait while preserving the remaining source facts and state. Apply each exclusion only to its owning character and selected Profile, never to other people. Do not invent an opposite trait or rewrite stored character/state data. These are description constraints: do not copy them into `negative`, turn them into negated captions, or treat them as instructions to the image generator. An absent or empty `character_negative` adds no exclusion.

COMPOSITION AND VISIBILITY

Within each scene's supplied or source-grounded camera/crop, judge projected visibility before choosing detail. Prioritize the readable event and its ownership/contact, then visible identity and current outfit/hair. Keep local details when readable and important to identity or action; reduce other hidden or incidental profile microdetails without deleting source facts. Use only visible or coverage-defining clothing in `positive`. Put scene-wide location, shared objects and an anonymous permitted partner fragment in `scene`, and the named person's own traits and main pose/action in their `positive`. Honor the trusted bot policy, especially SINGLE named-subject/LoRA ownership and minimum connected off-frame partner anatomy; never promote an anonymous fragment to a named character. Do not change source intensity or conceal explicit contact with unsupported clothing, furniture, obstructions or a safer crop. Depict one physically coherent instant, not multiple motion stages.

REGIONAL SUBJECT FOCUS

When the active output contract uses integrated regions with two or more named characters, include `solo` once, without added weight, in each region's `character_prompt` if that region depicts only its named owner. This is a local one-subject instruction, not a claim that the whole image contains one person. Keep the complete scene-wide figure count, placement and interaction in `composition_prompt`; do not copy regional `solo` into either shared prompt or add regional person-count tags. Two people standing apart or facing each other across a table can each have local `solo` while the shared composition still describes both.

Judge each region from the complete physical event. If another person's visible body or contact-bearing fragment must be depicted within that region, omit local `solo` there. A hand reaching across the other person's body is different from two independently occupied regions. Preserve the required contact and natural body bounds; do not remove a participant, shrink a mask or change the event to qualify for `solo`. With only one named region, or without integrated regional output, retain the existing scene-wide solo rules instead of introducing regional `solo`: a single region may be rendered without a mask. Anonymous fragments still receive no identity, extra region or additional person count.

TAGS AND NATURAL LANGUAGE

ANIMA reads tags and natural-language captions as one conditioning prompt. Include detail that establishes readable identity, current state or the chosen visible event; other detail supplied in the brief/profile is not a requirement to enumerate it; supplied face and eye identity tags follow the preservation rule above. Use compact tags for visible identity, current wardrobe and the main pose/action, removing repetition and incidental refinements without dropping the visible garment structure defined above. Preserve the brief's action-defining support, body connection, surface orientation and target relation; these are not incidental posture nuances. Let the image model realize nonessential joint/finger refinements, fabric folds and decoration.

Source-established injury marks (wounds, bruises and scars) get only concise, non-redundant type/body-region tags in the affected character's `positive`. For this injury-only reduction, treat detailed injury narration in a prepared brief as reference to shorten, not wording to preserve. Keep its event, pose and camera geometry, but put no description of injury appearance or anatomical landmarks in `camera`, `scene` or `supplement`. In those prose fields, mention injuries only to explain a necessary physical action, contact or occlusion; preserve treatment mechanics without amplifying injury appearance.

Write `supplement` in coherent English to carry the chosen instant's source-grounded physical relations that compact tags do not express clearly: who acts on what, body support and connection, the acting surface's orientation toward its target, and necessary contact or occlusion. Use one connected description of the chosen instant, with enough context to bind the action to the right body or object. State each relation once where it reads best; do not repeat the same action across camera, tags and prose or recaption appearance, clothing, expression, lighting and scenery already conveyed. For an ongoing transfer, express the incoming/contact path and the receiving surface's direction explicitly; a held result or outgoing spill alone does not describe that transfer. Preserve these concrete relations from the source or supplied brief rather than reducing them back to an action label. A simple portrait may need only a gaze target or no supplement. Do not fill missing relationships with decorative detail or transfer omitted microdetails into prose. Let the scene determine the length, with no fixed tag count, ratio or sentence quota.

In `camera`, `scene`, `position` and `supplement`, identify people by their actual visible placement or pose, with a minimal established distinguishing trait if needed, rather than narrative names: e.g., "the girl on the left", "the seated girl", or "the girl" for an unambiguous single subject. Match these references to the actual composition consistently; do not invent a placement to replace a name. Identify an anonymous fragment by its visible region and frame entry, without assigning an identity. Keep required image identity/LoRA trigger tokens intact.

POSITIVE IMAGE TEXT

Describe the intended visible state directly in positive image text. Negative prompts are managed separately by the application from configured presets and direct user input. Do not output a `negative` field or generate negative tags, even when a reference policy includes negative-output instructions. Do not move exclusion lists or negated instructions into positive tags or composition prose. Apply character exclusions by omitting the excluded trait, without inventing its opposite.

OUTPUT FIELDS

The `scenes` array contains exactly one scene for EACH assigned slot, with these fields: `slot`, `camera`, `characters`, `scene`, `supplement`. Keep assigned registered characters in input order, followed by any separately depicted source-grounded NPCs. The `slot` value is the exact supplied Cxxx anchor string, not a numeric insertion position. Every character has exactly `name`, `positive`, `position` (strings). Copy each canonical name exactly into `name`; for an NPC use its role label. `positive` holds that person's final desired image tags. Keep `position` a short spatial placement hint only (e.g., left/right/center, foreground/background, relative placement), without appearance, outfit, or tags. Do not add `outfit_state`, profile_ref, history, state, plan_id or extra fields to an ordinary scene. Use English image-prompt content. No analysis or prose outside the descriptor.
