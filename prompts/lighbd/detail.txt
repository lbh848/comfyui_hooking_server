When `state` is absent, determine current outfit and hair from source chronology at this scene's own anchor, using prior saved memory and earlier context; apply Profile defaults only when narrative and memory are silent. When supplied, each character's separate `time_place.time` and `time_place.place` belong to this same SLOT. When the planned brief is absent, choose one concrete source-supported visible instant within each assigned anchor and decide its camera, crop, composition, pose and contact before writing tags. CHARACTER EXCLUSIONS

The selected Profile's `character_negative` lists user-specified traits to exclude from that character's image description. Apply each exclusion only to its owning character and selected Profile, never to other people. Do not invent an opposite trait or rewrite stored character/state data. These are description constraints: do not copy them into `negative`, turn them into negated captions, or treat them as instructions to the image generator. An absent or empty `character_negative` adds no exclusion.

TAGS AND NATURAL LANGUAGE

ANIMA reads tags and natural-language captions as one conditioning prompt. Include detail that establishes readable identity, current state or the chosen visible event; other detail supplied in the brief/profile is not a requirement to enumerate it; supplied face and eye identity tags follow the preservation rule above. Use compact tags for visible identity, current wardrobe and the main pose/action, avoiding synonym piles and incidental refinements. Preserve the brief's action-defining support, body connection, surface orientation and target relation; these are not incidental posture nuances. Let the image model realize nonessential joint/finger refinements, fabric folds and decoration.

Before writing image tags or captions, translate any remaining emotional metaphor in the brief into literal visible expression or behavior. Do not materialize it as an invented object or wearable item; preserve actual source- or state-established items.

Source-established injury marks (wounds, bruises and scars) get only concise, non-redundant type/body-region tags in the affected character's `positive`. Write `supplement` in coherent English to carry the brief's essential physical relations that compact tags do not express clearly: who acts on what, body support and connection, the acting surface's orientation toward its target, and necessary contact or occlusion. Use one connected description of the chosen instant, with enough context to bind the action to the right body or object. State each relation once where it reads best; do not repeat the same action across camera, tags and prose or recaption appearance, clothing, expression, lighting and scenery already conveyed. For an ongoing transfer, express the incoming/contact path and the receiving surface's direction explicitly; a held result or outgoing spill alone does not describe that transfer. Preserve these concrete relations from the brief rather than reducing them back to an action label. A simple portrait may need only a gaze target or no supplement. Do not fill missing relationships with decorative detail or transfer omitted microdetails into prose. Let the scene determine the length, with no fixed tag count, ratio or sentence quota.

In `camera`, `scene`, `position` and `supplement`, identify people by their actual visible placement or pose, with a minimal established distinguishing trait if needed, rather than narrative names: e.g., "the girl on the left", "the seated girl", or "the girl" for an unambiguous single subject. Match these references to the actual composition consistently; do not invent a placement to replace a name. Identify an anonymous fragment by its visible region and frame entry, without assigning an identity. Keep required image identity/LoRA trigger tokens intact.
