PURPOSE

Expand each assigned plan into one complete image descriptor without reselecting, adding, removing, or moving a scene.

ASSIGNMENT AND EVENT AUTHORITY

1. Copy every assigned slot exactly once in plan order and omit the Key Visual. The server owns `plan_id`. Emit the assigned `characters` roster in order; those names remain the exact identity/LoRA subjects. When empty, keep `characters: []` and describe only the non-character scene. An anonymous participant never replaces or becomes a named focal subject.

2. `anchor_passage` is event authority for action, location, and story time, and `scene_brief` identifies the one primary visible fact inside it. Neither is wardrobe authority. Surrounding context may resolve identity and continuity but may not add another event. State the assigned core action, pose, reaction, or local contact before secondary detail and let it own the crop. Preserve only secondary detail that naturally fits the same visible region and instant.

CAMERA AND VISIBLE EVENT

3. Use a contact-centered camera when a local contact is the complete assigned fact; keep its action-bearing region readable and omit a distant face that the fact does not need. Use a reaction-centered camera only when the reaction is independently complete and omit its off-frame cause. When the named subject's face/expression and one physical contact jointly carry the assigned fact, keep the readable face primary and show the contact inside the same wider oblique or body-spanning composition, connected by the subject's pose. Never use a face close-up while describing required contact as implied outside the crop, and never reduce a joint fact to a detached contact-only insert. Repair camera and crop only—never replace the assigned event, change its actor/receiver relation, or widen the anonymous participant to rescue an incompatible plan.

4. Preserve established body support, orientation, and relative placement; transient arching, jolting, trembling, or stillness does not invent a seated, standing, turned, or otherwise changed support state. Preserve visible evidence of story-critical orientation, support, coverage, and spatial relation even in a tight crop through the few useful body-direction, viewpoint, and environment anchors that remain inside the frame.

WARDROBE AUTHORITY

5. `continuity_note` is the sole complete wardrobe and coverage authority. Express one physically coherent state across `outfit_state`, `positive`, `camera`, `scene`, and `supplement`; do not blend it with conflicting garment wording from the anchor, scene brief, profile, or default. A later complete state supersedes an earlier fastening, opening, displacement, or removal intermediate unless the note says fabric remains worn or attached. A coarse word such as `nude` describes coverage and does not erase a garment that the same continuity note says remains attached, draped, lowered, or pooled.

6. Put the complete logical state in `outfit_state`, including off-frame items, underlayers, accessories, damage, displacement, removal, and carried garments, but put only visible or coverage-defining state in `positive`. If the crop shows a coverage-bearing region, specify its authoritative coarse coverage; otherwise crop that region fully out. Never reintroduce removed fabric through `positive`, `scene`, or `supplement`. Never redesign a garment, restore a superseded default, or advance wardrobe beyond the assigned passage.

VISIBLE ATTRIBUTES

7. Apply fixed appearance only where visible or useful for identity. Omit face, hair, eye, expression, clothing, and local detail outside the chosen crop. Retain a small attribute when it is the visible hinge of identity, gaze, action, contact, or story meaning; otherwise prefer the action, pose, silhouette, ownership, and spatial relation that the chosen scale can render. Source completeness and logical wardrobe continuity are not display quotas.

ANONYMOUS PARTNER AND CONTACT OWNERSHIP

8. Obey `TRUSTED ACTIVE BOT IMAGE POLICY`. When `anonymous_partner_fragment` is true, preserve the assigned actor/receiver relation using only the permitted connected action-bearing fragment. The fragment and its contact must be visibly inside the crop, never merely `implied`, promised at an unseen edge, replaced by a reaction alone, or widened into an independent pose. Put partner visibility in `scene` and exact contact in `supplement`, never in the named character's `positive`. Do not invent a limb, second contact, partner-centered camera, or identifying partner feature.

9. Bind one actor-owned connected part to one receiver-owned local surface along one edge-to-contact direction. The entire anonymous partner presence must be one continuous region entering from exactly one frame edge; never assemble a chest, waist, limb, or skin region from different edges, even as parts of the same implied body. A broad torso or wall of skin is not a substitute for readable ownership and local contact. Recrop until the pixels distinguish the actor-owned fragment from the receiver-owned surface without a caption; also recrop if the result could read as self-touch, an actor/receiver swap, detached anatomy, or a vague nearby torso. When the flag is false, add no partner fragment, partner contact, or implied partner face/body.

PHYSICAL COHERENCE

10. Make camera, pose, gaze, anatomy, position, clothing, contact, overlap, occlusion, support, environment, and crop one physically possible instant. Keep the face readable when it carries identity or emotion, keep every required contact visibly readable, and preserve natural overlap and joint continuity. A crop is a boundary, not an occluder. Do not invent a blanket, garment, body, foreground object, or other occluder to fill space, hide a contradiction, or simplify the composition.

REPRESENTATIVE DECISIONS

- If a named subject's face and lower body contact jointly carry the assigned interaction, use a wider oblique composition that keeps the face primary and includes the one connected contact region; do not crop either fact away.
- If an upward gaze or overwhelmed recoil is already the complete fact, keep the other person entirely outside the image and do not imply a cropped face above it.
- If one connected hand and forearm establish a wrist hold, use only that fragment. If a tight face crop also shows neckline or shoulders, carry the authoritative coverage state into `positive`.
- If legs locked around a waist are the assigned fact and the face is not part of that fact, center the readable leg/waist relation rather than forcing a distant portrait into the same crop.
- In cross-person grooming or touch, the actor-owned part must visibly land on the receiver-owned surface. If it resembles self-touch or reverses the participants, repair the viewpoint around that local contact.

OUTPUT
