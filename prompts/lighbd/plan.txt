SCENE BRIEF

Read each assigned anchor in context and elaborate its selected action, reaction or relationship as one visible instant, not a sequence. Preserve the distinct intentions of the assigned scenes; invisible thoughts and abstract effects are not filler. Ground actor/receiver roles in the source, then stage the action as one coherent physical instant before choosing the camera. Describe only the relations needed for that action to work: where the body is supported, how the acting body region connects to it, the acting surface's orientation relative to its target, and contact or depth order where it matters. Commit to one posture and spatial arrangement, without alternative poses. When support, orientation or transfer direction determines whether the action works, state that relation concretely instead of leaving it implicit in the action name. For an ongoing transfer, describe the incoming/contact path and receiving orientation, not just an object held afterward or an outgoing spill. A surface catching something falling faces upward toward its arrival; a container being emptied directs its opening toward the destination. Keep the source-side fragment or incoming path and receiver readable in the same view, within the active visibility policy. A resting portrait may need only posture and gaze. Choose a viewpoint and crop that make those essential relations readable together while representing the assigned identities under the active policy. Do not sacrifice the action geometry merely to display the face, scenery or every available detail. Include established place/time and salient visible state only when useful; do not invent an event or enumerate joint angles, fingers or incidental pose refinements. A partner fragment belongs in this prose, not another field. This brief is the whole scene handoff to DETAIL: communicate the event and its minimum connected geometry, then useful visible facts.

Write the brief as literal visual description, not figurative narration. Express emotions through source-consistent visible facial expressions, gaze, posture and actions. Replace emotional metaphors with that visible behavior, even when the source uses figurative wording. An emotional metaphor does not establish a physical object, wardrobe item or visual effect. Include objects, clothing and accessories only when actually established in the source or authoritative current state.

Describe wounds, bruises, scars and other injury marks with only a short source-supported type and general visible body region. Keep the camera and visual priority on the selected action or reaction; narration dwelling on a mark does not make that mark the selected action. When injury or treatment is the selected event, retain only the source-supported mechanics needed to understand that event.

Preserve explicit source intensity and exposure, never conceal or euphemize it. Do not invent a place, time, contact or participant absent from the source. Make the selected event physically understandable under the active partner-visibility policy through natural crop, pose and connected anatomy. Refine the staging while preserving the supplied selection intent. Do not enlarge a partner beyond the active policy, move to another anchor or force distant contact and the subject's face into one close crop. Describe an instant, not the entire character's outfit, and do not reselect Profiles or override supplied tracked states.
